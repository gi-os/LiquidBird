# LiquidBird

Theos tweak injected into the NeoFreeBird IPA (Twitter-branded sideload IPA from the latest NeoFreeBird release: BHTwitter, branding already reverted; v7.3.0 = Twitter 12.38.1, built with the iOS 27.1 SDK). Personal sideload only.

## Goals
1. Real iOS 26 Liquid Glass: floating glass pill/rail for the tab bar, glass on nav bars and post-detail buttons. X ships this itself (XNavigation.TabBarPill, XDesignSystem.Glass, TFNGlass*) behind the `ios_liquid_glass_*` feature switches; NeoFreeBird forces them with its Labs toggle (`liquid_glass` in NSUserDefaults), which LiquidBird defaults on. Hook glass in ourselves only where X's redesign leaves gaps.
2. Remove Grok everywhere: tab, per-tweet button, post-detail floating button, "..." menu items.
3. Remove Live / Spaces: NeoFreeBird's hide_spaces (the Spaces line under Home's tabs, T1FleetLineHeaderController) and the live sports carousel (Tweak.x). Not yet: live broadcast cards in timelines (T1BroadcastCellCardView; hiding the card alone would likely leave a gap the cell's height, so check a layout copy first).
4. Ads, premium upsells and banners: rely on NeoFreeBird's BHTwitter toggles first; hook only what survives.

## Layout
- `Tweak.x`             Grok removal (controls whose label has Grok as a word, not inside a name or @handle, or whose identifier mentions it; tabs, menu items, X's `ios_tab_bar_default_show_grok` switch; a control hidden for Grok stays hidden whatever X asks, `UIControl -setHidden:`, and shows again as X last asked if X reuses it for something else); the status bar hidden while X's full-screen media viewer (T1ImmersiveFullScreenViewController) is open, folded too, changed only once it has finished opening or closing, and answered by the XNAVNavigationController wrapper it's presented in, which owns the status bar; defaults for NeoFreeBird's own toggles (liquid_glass, hide_quick_compose, hide_spaces, Grok, ads) via registerDefaults, so what Giovanni picks in its settings still wins (the sidebar's logo opens them); collapses the live sports carousel
- `Tiling.x`            the iPhone Duo, folded and unfolded (a window >= 600pt wide, or one with the status bar's side strip, `LBSideStrip`; ordinary iPhones held upright stay stock): Twitter for iPad's layout after gi-os/twitter's TabStackView/ClassicSidebar, in Liquid Glass. A glass column in the trailing strip the status bar runs down: the Twitter logo (tap: NeoFreeBird's settings, BHTManager BHTSettingsWithAccount:, whose own Settings row doesn't show on these layouts; long-press: the slide-over menu, which the hidden Home top bar's avatar opened, Explore beside the feed, the feed's place), then a rail of the tabs (Grok left out) ending in compose as its last row unless NeoFreeBird's `hide_tweet_button` is on (XTabbedAppNavigationViewController handleComposeTweetKeyCommand; X's own + fades out with the whole TabBarChromeContainerView, since its glass container draws the + whatever the button's alpha). The rail mirrors X's glass tab bar (XNavigation TabBarItemView, selected with accessibilityActivate), which fades out meanwhile; with Liquid Glass off X builds the older T1TabbedAppNavigationViewController, the rail mirrors its T1TabBarViewController, and Columns.x (which works on XNavigation's TabBarController) stays off. Home's top bar and an opened tweet's go (not in a column, whose bar holds its ✕); photo carousels scroll their last photo out from under the strip, and where X centres its column in a wide canvas (unfolded, feed full width) they're laid out as if the column ran on to the canvas's edge: photos sized for that width but none past X's per-photo cap in the column's carousel view (maxItemWidthRatio of the column, past which X crops it) and never shorter than X's own, the scroll view to the edge, scrolling where they still overrun it (T1StatusViewAttachmentLayoutSpec's bleed, T1StandardStatusAttachmentViewAdapter's carousel layout; the post keeps the column's width), and touches past the cell's content view reach them (T1StatusCell hitTest). X's own screens (T1HostViewController's child) end where the strip begins (`LBFitContent`), so what X lays out from its view's width rather than the safe area (an article's floating action bar, scroll bars, docks) doesn't run on under the sidebar; an article's action bar (InlineActionsFloatingBar) also keeps to the safe area wherever its view does reach the strip. As a photo closes, X scrolls the list to bring it into view (`tfn_programmaticallySetContentOffset:animated:` from `prepareMediaOriginForDismissal`) counting its faded tab bar's band as covered; that part of the scroll is dropped (`PhotoReturn`; the layout copy logs the Home timeline's position at each step of a photo opening and closing). Step 2 is `Columns.x`.
  The Duo folded is told by its status bar's strip down the trailing edge (`LBSideStrip`), remembered for each window size it's been seen at (or that size turned: folded, a photo turned with the phone makes the window 678x466): hiding the status bar (the media viewer, the slide-over menu) takes the strip's inset away, so T1HostViewController's additional safe area stands in for it meanwhile and nothing under it relayouts (that had scrolled the timeline). Each status bar change also puts Home back at the navigation's own frame (`LBShiftHome` moves it back, and measures the timeline's extra inset only on a later layout, once its safe area describes where it is; `LBCFitScreen` keeps it narrowed during a presentation, only a push or pop is left alone). `LBSidebarStrip` (the strip, else 66pt) is the sidebar's width for both files. Folded, the app stays upright (the screen says which pose); a photo over it can still turn.
- `Columns.x`           step 2 of the tiling, where there's a 400pt column plus a 160pt sliver beside the sidebar (the Duo unfolded: the window's short side >= 600pt, since folded a photo turned sideways makes it 678pt wide): pushes on a tab's stack (`XNAVNavigationController pushViewController:animated:`) open as columns in a strip over the tab's root instead (gi-os/twitter's TabStackView): after the column last touched, replacing later ones, newest at the trailing edge, snapping to column edges. Each column is an LBColumnNavigationController (a plain UINavigationController: creating X's XNAVNavigationController outside its tabs crashed) with a close button, in a container view controller under the app's root (T1HostViewController), below the sidebar, not in X's TabBarController, which expects only its own tabs; a push inside a column opens the next one, and if opening a column throws, the push goes ahead as usual. Leaving the tab or opening the slide-over menu closes them; folding pushes them back onto the stack (columns from the Explore panel onto the tab that's showing). Tapping outside the columns (the dimmed tab) or swiping them away (left past the end; right past the start when they're on the right) closes them all. With 600pt beside the sidebar the selected tab narrows (its bars through the safe area's sides; its screen is placed in its side outright, `LBCFitScreen`, since X's timelines and Explore's pages sit in paging cells that drop a safe area's sides) and X's own Explore tab (GuideContainerViewController in its XNAV, which X keeps loaded and hidden behind the selected tab) is shown as the panel on the feed's other side, like X desktop's right sidebar; `UILayoutContainerView -setHidden:` keeps it shown while it's the panel. X makes a tab the first time it's shown; until Explore has been opened the panel's place says to open it once (switching tabs on its behalf at launch broke Home's first load). As the panel its bar's buttons (gear, +) go. Wherever there's room the feed is narrow; a two-finger swipe on the tabs moves it a step left or right, cancelling X's own page swipes under the fingers (or the sidebar logo's long-press menu picks): left, full width (X's own feed, no Explore panel) or right (`liquidbird_feed_side`, -1/0/1; older builds kept `liquidbird_feed_right`), columns opening over the other side (open ones move with it), and the menu turns the Explore panel off (`liquidbird_explore_panel`), leaving black. Not yet: pull-down to close, wide web panels, the fold (book pose).

- `scripts/patch_glass.sh <in.ipa> <out.ipa>`  strips UIDesignRequiresCompatibility so stock UIKit chrome gets Liquid Glass; raises the Mach-O SDK to 26.0 only if it's older (never lowers it, keeps minos). Needs Xcode `vtool`, macOS only

Never a public release.

- `scripts/register_link.sh [minutes]`  on the Mac mini: one-time link that captures a new device's UDID; then `gh workflow run build.yml -f udid=<udid> -f device_name=<name>` registers it and re-signs.

## Rules
- Class names marked `// placeholder` are unverified. Confirm in FLEX before relying on them: the IPA already ships BHTwitter's FLEX (`libbhFLEX.dylib`, try its toggle in BHTwitter settings first), else inject FLEXing (`scripts/build.sh --flex <FLEXing.deb>`, or run the workflow with `flex`). Placeholder hooks live in their own `%group`, initialised in `%ctor` only if `objc_getClass` finds the class; keep it that way (Logos doesn't check, and a nil class can crash at launch). Prefer matching on accessibilityLabel/identifier over private class names; they survive app updates.
- Guard every iOS 26 API with `if (@available(iOS 26, *))`. Deployment target stays 15.0.
- Following X's code: `scripts/objc_find.py <macho> <selector-regex>` gives a method's address, `scripts/objc_dis.py <macho> <start> <end>` disassembles it with the messages it sends and the classes and strings it loads (how the full-screen viewer's status bar owner, its XNAV wrapper, was found).
- Finding classes: `nm`/strings miss most of them; `scripts/objc_dump.py <macho> <class-regex>` dumps ObjC metadata (class and category method lists; `--names` in place of the regex lists every class; run with `python3 -I`) from the frameworks in the NeoFreeBird IPA (T1Twitter, XServiceLibraries, XAppLibraries hold the app). Navigation (Liquid Glass, which LiquidBird forces; X's T1MainAppNavigationProviderFactory picks it when XAppearance's isLiquidGlassEnabled, iOS 26 on): T1HostViewController > XTabbedAppNavigationViewController (`handleComposeTweetKeyCommand`, `presentDashFromViewController:animated:completion:`, `isDashOpen`, `dismissDashAnimated:completion:`) > TFNTopDockedBarContainerViewController > T1AppSplitViewController > XNavigation.TabBarController > one XNAVNavigationController per tab (`pushViewController:animated:`, which Columns.x hooks); the tab bar is XNavigation.TabBarView/TabBarItemView/TabBarPill in TabBarChromeContainerView, compose is TabBarAccessoryButtonView or TFNFloatingActionButton (there either way). With Liquid Glass off X builds T1TabbedAppNavigationViewController (`currentTabNavigationController`), T1TabBarViewController (`tabViews`, `setTabBarHidden:withDuration:completion:`), TFNCustomTabBar and T1TabNavigationController (`showComposeFromBarButtonItem:`) instead; Tiling.x's `Tiling` group is only for that.
- No global hooks on `UIView -layoutSubviews`; too hot. `UIControl` and `UIScrollView -didMoveToWindow` are the widest acceptable hooks.
- Never commit IPAs, decrypted binaries, or signing certs. `.gitignore` covers them.
- The repo is public (free macOS minutes), so the signed IPA (its ad-hoc profile lists the devices) goes up encrypted to the Mac mini: a one-time key encrypted to `artifact-key.pub.pem`, whose private half is only `~/.config/liquidbird/artifact-key.pem` there (`scripts/install_link.sh` decrypts). The unsigned IPA between jobs is NeoFreeBird's public app plus this tweak, and is deleted once signed. Logs must not list devices; a UDID passed as a dispatch input shows in that run.
- Signing secrets (synced from the gi-os/ios-certs hub) go only to the `sign` job, which runs no third-party code; never pass them to `ipa`. Registered so far: Giovanni's iPhone and iPad; the ad-hoc profile lasts until Sep 2027.

## Commands
- `make package FINALPACKAGE=1`  builds `packages/*.deb`
- `./scripts/build.sh`           end to end, output `out/LiquidBird.ipa`
- `./scripts/syntax_check.sh`    compile check in seconds before pushing: each .x as plain ObjC (`scripts/delogos.py`) through clang -Werror against the iOS SDK
